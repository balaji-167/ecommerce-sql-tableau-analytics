# Business Insights

## Executive Summary

This e-commerce analytics project analyzes revenue, product profitability, customer health, retention, funnel conversion, geography, and user behaviour.

The overall business story is clear:

> The business is generating steady revenue with healthy product economics, but weak retention and customer reactivation are limiting durable growth.

The company has decent acquisition and conversion performance, but it appears to rely heavily on continuously replacing lost or inactive customers rather than compounding value from existing customers.

---

## 1. Executive Overview

### Key Metrics

| Metric | Value |
|---|---:|
| Total Revenue | $4.49M |
| Total Orders | 33,580 |
| Total Customers | 16,268 |
| Average Order Value | $133.81 |
| Median Order Value | $86.46 |
| Revenue per Customer | $276.20 |

### Insight

The average order value is significantly higher than the median order value. This suggests the order distribution is right-skewed, meaning a smaller number of high-value orders are pulling the average upward.

### Business Interpretation

The business likely has two customer realities:

- A premium/high-value customer segment that drives larger baskets
- A broader everyday customer base with more modest order values

Leadership should avoid relying only on average order value because it may overstate the typical customer transaction.

## 3. Retention Is the Biggest Strategic Weakness

### Key Retention Signals

| Metric | Value |
|---|---:|
| Repeat Customer Rate | 61.75% |
| Single-Order Customer Rate | 38.25% |
| Churned Customer Rate | 91.06% |
| Active Customer Rate | 2.96% |

Average cohort retention is also low after acquisition:

| Month | Avg Retention Rate |
|---|---:|
| Month 1 | 2.33% |
| Month 3 | 2.50% |
| Month 6 | 2.45% |
| Month 12 | 2.43% |

### Insight

After initial purchase, very few customers appear to remain active in later monthly cohort periods.

### Business Interpretation

The business can still look healthy in revenue dashboards while hiding a structural retention problem underneath. If customers do not return consistently, revenue becomes dependent on continuous acquisition.

This creates risk because:

- Customer acquisition costs can become harder to recover
- Customer lifetime value remains limited
- Future revenue growth becomes less predictable
- Paid acquisition efficiency becomes fragile

## 5. Customer Health and Churn Risk

The churn analysis shows a large number of customers classified as at risk, dormant, or lost, with significant revenue tied to those customers.

### Insight

The business has a major reactivation opportunity.

### Business Interpretation

If the company already paid to acquire these customers, allowing them to lapse without a structured recovery program creates avoidable revenue loss.

### Recommended Customer Recovery Strategy

#### Tier 1: High-Value Lapsed Customers

Target customers with:

- High lifetime revenue
- Multiple historical orders
- Recently lapsed status

Recommended actions:

- Personalized win-back campaigns
- Product recommendations based on previous purchases
- Urgency-based offers
- Higher-touch outreach for valuable customers

#### Tier 2: Second-Purchase Rescue Segment

Target customers who:

- Have placed only one order
- Purchased recently
- Have not made a second purchase

Goal:

> Convert one-time buyers into repeat customers.

#### Tier 3: Long-Lapsed Low-Value Customers

Use lower-cost automated campaigns:

- Broad promotions
- Bundles
- Seasonal reminders
- Category nudges

## 6. Funnel Conversion Analysis

### Funnel Metrics

| Stage | Sessions | Overall Conversion |
|---|---:|---:|
| Page View | 120,000 | 100.00% |
| Add to Cart | 81,518 | 67.93% |
| Checkout | 44,909 | 37.42% |
| Purchase | 33,580 | 27.98% |

### Step Conversion

| Step | Conversion |
|---|---:|
| Page View → Add to Cart | 67.93% |
| Add to Cart → Checkout | 55.09% |
| Checkout → Purchase | 74.77% |

### Insight

The biggest drop occurs between Add to Cart and Checkout.

### Business Interpretation

Users are showing product intent by adding items to cart, but many do not proceed to checkout. This may indicate friction such as shipping cost surprises, weak cart UX, low urgency, trust concerns, or comparison shopping.

### Recommended Actions

- Improve cart page clarity
- Show total cost earlier
- Add delivery and returns messaging
- Improve abandoned cart recovery
- Use threshold incentives instead of blanket discounts
- Optimize mobile cart experience

---

## 7. Source and Device Conversion

The source-device heatmap shows conversion rates mostly in the high-20% range.

### Key Insight

Social + Tablet showed the strongest purchase conversion rate at approximately 30.3%.

### Business Interpretation

Mobile and tablet experiences are important for conversion. Email and owned channels may also be underused opportunities because they often convert efficiently.

### Recommended Actions

- Analyze conversion by traffic source, device, AOV, and repeat behaviour
- Improve mobile and tablet checkout experience
- Invest more in high-converting owned channels
- Connect source/device performance with CLV for better marketing allocation

---

## 8. Geography Performance

The United States is the largest revenue market, but revenue is not fully dependent on one geography.

### Key Insight

The US contributed approximately $823K and around 18.33% of total revenue.

### Business Interpretation

International demand is real, which creates opportunities for localized market strategies.

### Recommended Actions

- Build localized campaigns for top markets
- Improve country-specific shipping and trust messaging
- Review payment preferences by region
- Identify high-AOV and high-revenue-per-customer countries

## 10. Product-Level Action Matrix

The product profitability dashboard supports a practical assortment strategy.

### Scale

Products with:

- High revenue
- High profit
- Solid reviews
- Repeatable demand

Recommended action:

- Increase visibility
- Improve inventory confidence
- Promote through remarketing
- Use in bundles or campaigns

### Fix

Products with:

- High revenue but weak margin
- Poor ratings
- Weak conversion

Recommended action:

- Review pricing
- Improve product content
- Negotiate cost
- Test bundling

### Nurture

Products with:

- High margin
- Low volume

Recommended action:

- Increase exposure
- Test promotions
- Improve product placement

### Exit or Deprioritize

Products with:

- Low revenue
- Low profit
- Weak reviews
- Weak strategic fit

Recommended action:

- Reduce focus
- Review inventory strategy
- Consider discontinuation

## 11. Reviews and Ratings

Product ratings are generally acceptable, but review volume may be underused as a conversion lever.

### Insight

Some products generate strong revenue despite limited review density.

### Business Interpretation

Increasing review volume for top products could improve product trust, add-to-cart confidence, and checkout conversion.

### Recommended Actions

- Prioritize review generation for top-revenue products
- Trigger review requests after delivery
- Highlight review count and rating together
- Use review content in product pages and remarketing

---

## 12. User Path Analysis

Key paths include:

- Page View → Page View
- Page View → Add to Cart
- Add to Cart → Page View
- Checkout → Purchase

### Insight

Heavy Page View → Page View movement suggests browsing loops before conversion.

### Business Interpretation

This may indicate healthy product exploration, but it can also suggest friction in product discovery, navigation, comparison, or decision-making.

### Recommended Actions

- Improve category navigation
- Review product detail page clarity
- Analyze mobile browsing depth
- Investigate add-to-cart reversal behaviour
- Use session replay or product analytics for deeper diagnosis

---

## 13. Priority Business Problems

The dashboard suggests these problems should be solved in this order:

1. Retention is too weak
2. Cart-to-checkout leakage is expensive
3. Revenue growth is plateauing
4. Middle-segment customer value is not expanding enough
5. Product and review signals are not fully monetized

---

## 14. 90-Day Action Plan

### First 30 Days: Stop the Biggest Leaks

- Launch win-back campaigns for high-value lapsed customers
- Build second-purchase campaigns for one-time buyers
- Improve cart abandonment flows
- Start review request campaigns for top products

### Days 30–60: Segment and Personalize

Create customer segments:

- High-CLV active customers
- High-CLV at-risk customers
- Recent one-time buyers
- Repeat low-value customers
- Repeat high-value customers

Use these segments to personalize:

- Offers
- Timing
- Product recommendations
- Messaging channels

### Days 60–90: Optimize Economics

Focus on:

- Source/device quality
- Category hero products
- SKU pruning
- Country-level conversion opportunities
- Repeat purchase uplift measurement

---

## Final Business Conclusion

This is a solid e-commerce business with healthy product economics and decent conversion performance, but weak retention is preventing durable growth.

The best growth lever is not only more traffic. The strongest opportunity is:

> Better reactivation, stronger second-purchase conversion, and smarter lifecycle monetization of customers the business has already acquired.
